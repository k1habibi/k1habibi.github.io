What I work on

Reservoirs in arid regions are monitored with few sensors and short records. I build models that make the most of that data, respect the physics of a stratified water column and say how sure they are.

Physics-guided causal deep learning DAG

Networks shaped by known cause and effect, so predictions keep physically sensible responses and come with calibrated uncertainty.

Explainable water quality indices XAI

Indices whose weights and class decisions trace back to the indicators that drive them, using SHAP and LIME.

Reservoir digital twins 3D + t

Time-varying three-dimensional models of a stratified supply reservoir, built from repeated CTD profiling campaigns.

Remote sensing below the surface UAV · S2

Drone multispectral and thermal imagery, Sentinel-2 and Landsat linked to profiler casts to estimate chlorophyll the sensors cannot see.

Water Research · to be submittedfirst author

A transferable pipeline for chlorophyll-a

A soft-causal, physics-guided deep learning pipeline built on 1,012 Mexican inland-water stations and re-run on an Omani reservoir. Its predictions stayed physically plausible far more often than those of tree ensembles.

4,163 Mexican samples · 8,810 reservoir depth bins from 17 campaigns
J. Cleaner Production · to be submittedfirst author

Dropping indicators biases the CCME index

A Canadian (CCME) water quality index recomputed from three of seven indicators matched only 57% of the full index's classes. An evidence-anchored reduced index matched 97%, provided light was still measured in each profile.

684 depth layers · 17 profiler surveys · 127 indicator subsets tested
Remote Sensing of Environment · in preparationfirst author

How deep can remote sensing see?

Five drone multispectral and thermal surveys, Sentinel-2 and Landsat imagery and 501 CTD casts. In this reservoir about 90% of the chlorophyll in the sunlit layer lay below what the sensors see.
